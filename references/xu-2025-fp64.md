## Question and setting

Whether known PINN failure modes come from bad local minima or from floating-point precision. Uses the standard failure benchmarks (convection, reaction, wave) with 101 x 101 collocation points and L-BFGS.

## Claims we may use

- Abstract and Section 5.2: in FP32, L-BFGS "prematurely satisfies its convergence test"; in FP64 the same vanilla PINNs train past the failure phase.
- Section 5.2: the cause is that PyTorch L-BFGS `tolerance_change` = 1e-7 is smaller than FP32 machine epsilon (1.19e-7), so the stop test fires on rounding noise.
- Section 5.5 and Table 2: on an NVIDIA H100 PCIe, FP64 time per iteration was about 1.1 to 1.3 times FP32, and memory about 1.5 to 1.7 times.

## Relevance to this project

Directly affects our precision choice. If we train in FP32 with PyTorch L-BFGS, an early stop can look like convergence. The 1.1 to 1.3x overhead was measured on a data-center GPU with strong FP64 units; it does not carry over to a consumer GPU, where FP64 throughput can be much lower. Our pilot must measure the FP32 versus FP64 cost on our own machine before choosing, and the choice changes S_P and e_P.
