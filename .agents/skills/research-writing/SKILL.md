# Research writing

Read the [project rules](../../../AGENTS.md) and [writing guide](../../../docs/writing-style.md). Read only the research plan or source passages needed for the current text. Do not load every project file for a short edit.

## Write

Use the owner's supplied wording when it fits the meaning. Otherwise choose simple, common words. Correct typing mistakes without replacing the direct voice. Do not infer the owner's vocabulary from previous AI-written drafts.

Before editing, note the facts and protected parts: speaker, methods, results, values, units, conditions, uncertainty, citations, equations, code, and exact quotations. Keep the distinction between a planned experiment and a measured result.

Draft with the humanlike-writing approach: answer directly, use specific actions, keep necessary technical terms, and remove words that add no information. Follow the required document format. Let sentence and paragraph length follow the thought rather than a pattern.

## Review

Review with the humanizer approach. Look for stock openings, inflated words, repeated frames, vague authority, forced contrasts, and empty conclusions. Remove them without adding a new fact or a fake personal opinion. Do not add em dashes to new prose.

If the two named skills are installed, load them. If not, this file and the writing guide contain the project's required process. Do not copy an example's statistics, sources, interviewees, or personal stories into our work.

Compare the final text with the input. Check every protected value and qualification. Never change "may" to "will" merely to sound confident. If the input lacks evidence, keep the gap visible rather than filling it with a plausible claim.

Return the final text in the requested format. Keep draft/audit steps private unless the user asks to see them or a factual problem needs explaining. Do not append a style report to exact-format output. Follow the teacher's AI-use and disclosure rules; do not claim human authorship or detector avoidance.
